# The Vision for SOLACE Network

### Live Well Together.

Imagine a world where families and neighbors have the tools to communicate, share knowledge, solve problems, and work together without depending entirely on large corporations or centralized services.

Imagine a neighborhood where people share gardening experiences, help one another learn new skills, conduct scientific research, exchange resources, and stay connected even when conventional communication networks are unavailable.

Imagine communities that can build and maintain their own infrastructure, using affordable equipment and software they can freely use, study, modify, and share.

**That's the vision behind SOLACE Network.**

## It Starts With You. All of Us. Together.

SOLACE Network begins with a simple idea: individuals and communities can accomplish more when they have the means to cooperate.

A person growing vegetables in their backyard might have something valuable to teach a neighbor. A retired engineer might help a community improve its energy efficiency. A parent might share educational resources with other families. An amateur naturalist might contribute observations to a larger scientific study.

Individually, these efforts may seem small. Together, they can become something much more significant.

SOLACE Network aims to provide a common framework for these activities, helping people organize information, communicate, and participate in projects that matter to them.

You don't need to be a scientist, computer programmer, or technology enthusiast to participate. The goal is to make useful tools accessible to ordinary people.

## Connecting Families and Neighborhoods

The household is the starting point for SOLACE Network.

Every household is different. Some contain one person, while others include several generations, children, extended families, or unrelated people sharing a home.

SOLACE recognizes that diversity.

Households can organize into neighborhoods, creating communities where people can share information and cooperate on local activities.

A neighborhood might use SOLACE to organize a community garden, share tools, coordinate volunteer projects, publish announcements, or simply help neighbors get to know one another.

Neighborhoods are intended to organize themselves, with participating households having an equal voice in community decisions.

The goal isn't to impose a single model of community life. It's to provide the tools that allow communities to decide what works best for them.

## Technology That Belongs to the People Using It

Much of modern life depends on services operated by a relatively small number of companies.

We communicate through their platforms, store information on their servers, and rely on their networks to reach one another.

These services can be extraordinarily useful. But what happens when they become unavailable, too expensive, restrictive, or no longer meet our needs?

SOLACE Network explores a different approach.

Instead of requiring everyone to depend permanently on one central service, we want to help people operate more of their own technology.

The initial SOLACE applications will use conventional internet infrastructure. Over time, we hope to make it possible for households and neighborhoods to run their own services and communicate directly with one another.

A community should eventually be able to maintain access to important local information and communicate with nearby participants even when its connection to the wider internet is interrupted.

This isn't about rejecting the internet or replacing every existing service.

**It's about having alternatives, maintaining independence, and giving communities more choices.**

## A Framework, Not a Walled Garden

SOLACE Network is intended to be open and modular.

People should be able to begin with a simple application and adopt additional services as their needs grow.

Some participants might use SOLACE only for gardening or education. Others might operate a household computer that provides local storage, communication, and access to shared community resources.

Neighborhoods may eventually build their own communication infrastructure, connecting homes without relying exclusively on commercial internet providers.

The long-term goal is to support many kinds of equipment and software, rather than forcing everyone to purchase a particular product.

A person who already owns suitable equipment should be able to use it.

Someone interested in building their own system should have access to the documentation and tools needed to do so.

And people who simply want useful applications should not need to understand how the underlying technology works.

## Learning and Sharing Knowledge

Knowledge becomes more valuable when it can be shared.

SOLACE Network envisions a growing collection of educational materials, reference information, and practical knowledge that households and communities can use.

This could include resources for home education, gardening, repairs, scientific research, emergency preparation, and everyday problem-solving.

Some information could be stored locally, remaining available even when internet access is unreliable.

A community might create its own collection of instructional materials, while a household might maintain educational resources for children or adults learning new skills.

Our goal is to make knowledge easier to preserve, organize, and share.

## Community Science: Everyone Can Contribute

Scientific discovery isn't limited to universities and professional laboratories.

Gardeners, amateur astronomers, birdwatchers, naturalists, students, and curious individuals already make observations that can contribute to scientific understanding.

SOLACE Network aims to support this kind of community-driven research.

Consider gardening.

A person might record planting dates, soil conditions, rainfall, temperatures, pest activity, and harvest results.

Those observations can help improve their own garden. But imagine thousands of people voluntarily contributing comparable information over several years.

Researchers might be able to identify patterns involving climate, growing conditions, regional differences, and successful cultivation techniques.

Similar approaches could eventually support other fields, including local weather, environmental monitoring, and biodiversity research.

The broader vision is to help communities participate in meaningful scientific work while respecting the people who contribute the information.

We also envision a nonprofit SOLACE Foundation that could help steward collective research datasets, make them available for qualifying scientific and nonprofit work, and explore responsible commercial licensing that returns value to the project.

The details of data ownership, privacy, access, and licensing will require careful development before such a system is implemented.

## Stronger Communities Through Shared Resources

Not every household needs to own every tool, piece of equipment, or specialized resource.

Communities already share skills, lend equipment, exchange favors, and help neighbors in need.

SOLACE could make these activities easier to organize.

A neighborhood might maintain a shared collection of tools or educational materials. Residents could coordinate community projects, exchange useful information, or identify people willing to offer assistance.

Over time, applications could support local resource directories, community announcements, collaborative projects, and other practical services.

The purpose is not simply to create another social media platform.

It's to help people turn communication into useful cooperation.

**Social media with a purpose** was one of our earliest descriptions of this idea.

## Resilience and Local Independence

Modern communication networks are powerful, but they're not always available.

Storms, power failures, natural disasters, infrastructure damage, and other disruptions can interrupt access to services people depend on.

SOLACE Network's long-term vision includes helping neighborhoods maintain some communication and information services under those circumstances.

Households could operate local computers containing useful applications and reference materials. Nearby homes might communicate through community-operated wireless equipment.

In more advanced versions, separate neighborhoods might exchange information without needing a continuous connection to the commercial internet.

We also hope to encourage energy-efficient equipment, battery backup, and renewable power where practical.

This is a long-term direction, not a claim that SOLACE currently provides emergency communications or disaster-response infrastructure.

The underlying principle is simple: communities become more resilient when they have the knowledge and tools to maintain essential capabilities themselves.

## Local Communities, Connected Globally

SOLACE begins locally, but its ambitions extend beyond individual neighborhoods.

Imagine a community in North Carolina researching gardening techniques and sharing its findings with participants in another state or country.

Imagine groups of students exchanging educational resources, or communities comparing environmental observations across different climates.

Each neighborhood could retain its identity and independence while participating in a wider network of knowledge and cooperation.

The vision is a network built from the ground up, connecting people through their households and communities rather than requiring every interaction to pass through one central organization.

Independent communities should be able to cooperate without surrendering control over their own local affairs.

## A Network Shaped by Its Participants

We envision SOLACE Network growing into a community-supported project with transparent governance.

Participating households would have an equal voice, regardless of their size, wealth, technical resources, or length of membership.

The proposed SOLACE Foundation would help maintain shared standards, coordinate development, support research, and steward resources used for the benefit of the network.

Over time, the community itself should play an increasingly important role in shaping SOLACE's direction.

The details of this governance structure are still being developed.

Our objective is to build something that serves its participants rather than treating them merely as customers or sources of data.

## Starting Small, Thinking Long-Term

SOLACE Network is an ambitious project, and much of what we've described remains a long-term goal.

We don't expect to build everything at once.

The first steps involve creating practical applications, establishing household and neighborhood membership, and developing the foundation needed for future growth.

As the software matures, we'll explore local household services, compatible hardware, direct neighborhood communications, and eventually more decentralized networking.

Each stage should provide something useful on its own.

A gardening application should be worthwhile even if a neighborhood never installs wireless networking equipment. Educational tools should remain useful to people who only have a browser and an internet connection.

The larger network should grow from applications and services that people genuinely want to use, not from requiring everyone to adopt an entire technology ecosystem.

We expect the project to evolve as we learn, experiment, and discover new possibilities.

## A Template for Freedom

One of the earliest descriptions of SOLACE Network was **"a template for freedom."**

That remains a meaningful way to express our aspirations.

Not freedom from other people, but greater freedom to cooperate with them.

Freedom to learn, experiment, and share what we discover.

Freedom to operate our own tools and preserve our own knowledge.

Freedom to build stronger relationships within our communities.

Freedom to choose technologies that serve our needs rather than adapting our lives to the limitations of a particular platform.

SOLACE Network cannot guarantee these outcomes. But we believe an open framework, shaped by the people who use it, can help make them possible.

The vision is large. The starting point is small.

A person. A household. A neighborhood.

**It starts with you. All of us. Together.**
