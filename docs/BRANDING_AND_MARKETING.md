# Branding and Marketing for SOLACE Network

## 1. Purpose

This document collects the branding principles, visual identity, terminology, slogans, promotional ideas, and historical marketing concepts developed for SOLACE Network.

Its purpose is to provide a consistent foundation for future websites, software applications, documentation, hardware branding, and public outreach.

Not every concept recorded here has been approved for use. Historical ideas and possible future campaigns are preserved for reference.

The broader mission and long-term goals are described in [VISION.md](VISION.md).

## 2. Brand Identity

### 2.1 Official name

**SOLACE Network**

The preferred branding is "SOLACE Network" rather than "the SOLACE Network."

Avoid using an ampersand in official copy when "and" is appropriate.

### 2.2 Acronym

SOLACE originally stands for:

**Sustainable Open-Source Lab Augmented Community Example**

The acronym is part of the project's history and identity, but public messaging should prioritize explaining what SOLACE does rather than requiring audiences to remember the expanded name.

### 2.3 Brand philosophy

SOLACE Network emphasizes:

- Independence through open technology.
- Cooperation between individuals, households, and neighborhoods.
- Practical knowledge, teaching, and learning.
- Community participation and scientific discovery.
- Resilience and local self-reliance.
- Affordable, accessible tools.
- Freedom to choose compatible hardware and software.

The brand should communicate optimism, cooperation, curiosity, and practical empowerment.

SOLACE Network is intended to complement existing infrastructure while helping communities develop greater independence.

### 2.4 Public communication

Marketing should use understandable, everyday language.

For general audiences, terms such as *families*, *households*, *neighbors*, and *communities* are preferable to technical identifiers and networking acronyms.

Technical language belongs in engineering documentation and specialist materials.

Descriptions of future capabilities should distinguish aspirations from operational services. SOLACE should not claim to provide independent wireless connectivity, emergency communications, or guaranteed security before those capabilities are implemented and validated.

## 3. Primary Marketing Messages

The following are strong candidates for prominent branding.

### Live Well Together

An approachable, human-centered expression of SOLACE's mission.

It communicates the intended benefit of participation without requiring technical knowledge.

### It Starts With You. All of Us. Together.

Emphasizes individual initiative, collective participation, and cooperation.

This could serve as an overarching campaign slogan or closing statement in promotional materials.

Original wording:

> It starts with you. all of us. together.

### A Template for Freedom

An early description of the broader SOLACE philosophy.

It suggests that SOLACE provides tools and a framework that people can adapt to their own circumstances.

Freedom in this context means greater independence, choice, ownership, and the ability to cooperate without unnecessary dependence on centralized services.

### Connecting Families and Neighborhoods Together

One of the project's original descriptions.

A related historical variation:

> We're connecting families and neighborhoods together one by one.

This message is particularly suitable for neighborhood outreach and introductory materials.

### Social Media With a Purpose

An early explanation of SOLACE as a platform for meaningful community interaction.

It remains useful as a historical positioning statement, although SOLACE now encompasses more than social networking.

## 6. The SOLACE Symbol

### 6.1 Visual meaning

The SOLACE symbol represents connected but independent households or neighborhoods.

The design incorporates a stylized S, two dots, and a dividing boundary.

Its visual concept draws on balance and complementary relationships, with a resemblance to the yin-yang principle.

The symbol should communicate that separate communities can remain independent while cooperating as part of a larger network.

Official logo reference:

https://solace.network/solace-logo.svg

### 6.2 Simplicity and recognition

An important design objective is visual simplicity.

The symbol should be recognizable in small sizes and simple enough to reproduce on:

- Website graphics.
- Application icons.
- Hardware enclosures.
- Stickers and printed materials.
- Signs and community notices.
- Emergency-related signage, where appropriate.

The original concept included a symbol simple enough to spray-paint or stencil when necessary.

### 6.3 Animation

An earlier website concept proposed animating the symbol with one leftward rotation.

The symbol could also serve as a progress or loading indicator.

Animation should remain optional and should respect accessibility preferences, including reduced-motion settings.

## 7. Hardware and Compatibility Branding

SOLACE aims to encourage a vendor-neutral hardware ecosystem.

The official SOLACE OS and official reference devices may use dedicated SOLACE branding.

Third-party or self-built equipment that satisfies interoperability requirements may be described as:

**Compatible with SOLACE Network**

The distinction should be clear:

- Official devices represent approved SOLACE reference implementations.
- Compatible devices are independent implementations meeting published requirements.
- Purchasing official hardware is not necessary to participate.

### 7.1 Proposed hardware status indicator

An earlier branding concept proposed placing an illuminated lens behind a cutout of the SOLACE symbol.

Suggested colors included:

- **Green:** Internet connectivity available.
- **Yellow:** Local neighborhood connectivity available.
- **Red:** Low battery or emergency-only communication mode.

These are proposed indicator conventions, not finalized hardware specifications.

Technical details belong in [HARDWARE.md](HARDWARE.md).

### 7.2 Trademark considerations

Earlier discussions considered reserving particular official device marks while permitting descriptive compatibility labeling.

The goal is to protect the identity of official SOLACE equipment without preventing third-party interoperability.

Potential uses such as shirts, stickers, and bumper stickers were also discussed.

Trademark and permitted-use policies have not been finalized.

## 8. Application Branding

SOLACE applications may share a consistent visual identity while retaining distinct names and purposes.

Previously discussed application families include:

- Home
- Garden
- Library
- Neighborhood
- Education
- Network

Application names should remain understandable in ordinary text, documentation, accessibility tools, and website addresses.

Distinctive typography, icons, or SVG wordmarks can supply additional visual identity without relying on decorative Unicode characters.

**Shared identity does not require a shared visual template.** Garden may use a gardening-focused visual language, while Library, Neighborhood, Network, Home, and Education use interfaces suited to their tasks. A common SOLACE mark, recognizable naming and optional return-to-SOLACE navigation can establish family resemblance without imposing identical toolbars, colors, window frames, or component libraries. Historical simulated desktop-window styling is not an organization-wide brand requirement.

Individual application repositories may maintain their own detailed branding specifications.

## 9. Marketing Campaign Concepts

### 9.1 Recognition through curiosity

One proposed approach is to introduce the SOLACE symbol through repeated public exposure before audiences fully understand its meaning.

The intention is to create curiosity and recognition.

Historical inspirations discussed included:

- Seeing the Van Halen logo painted on buildings before recognizing it as a band's symbol.
- The "Free Kevin" bumper sticker campaign associated with 2600 magazine and Kevin Mitnick.
- Curiosity-driven messages that encourage people to discover the story behind a recognizable symbol.

These are historical marketing inspirations rather than endorsements of unauthorized graffiti.

### 9.2 Symbol-based campaigns

Potential campaigns could incorporate the SOLACE symbol into words and concepts associated with the project.

Examples discussed include:

- Sustainability.
- Science.
- Sharing.

The concept is to make the symbol recognizable across different subjects while maintaining a consistent visual identity.

### 9.3 Stickers and informal promotion

Stickers, bumper stickers, posters, and other inexpensive physical materials could help establish recognition.

One humorous concept recalled from an earlier discussion was:

**FREE KEVIN!***

With very small text reading:

*With qualifying purchase of Kevin of equal or greater value.*

This was a historical joke inspired by the earlier campaign, not a proposed official SOLACE slogan.

### 9.4 Community recruitment

Neighborhood outreach may eventually use:

- Email invitations.
- Shareable links.
- QR codes.
- Printable flyers.
- Posters and local notices.
- Word-of-mouth invitations.

Messaging should emphasize practical benefits and community participation rather than technical specifications.

Recruitment ideas specific to neighborhood registration are also documented in [NEIGHBORHOOD_MEMBERSHIP.md](NEIGHBORHOOD_MEMBERSHIP.md).

## 10. Communication Themes

### 10.1 Independence without isolation

SOLACE should not be presented as rejecting existing internet services or modern technology.

The objective is to provide choices and alternatives while retaining the ability to cooperate with the wider world.

### 10.2 Technology with a practical purpose

Promotional materials should demonstrate tangible benefits.

Gardening, education, local communication, neighborhood cooperation, and community research provide understandable examples.

### 10.3 Open participation

The project is intended to be free to join and usable without purchasing special equipment.

Advanced hardware and infrastructure may be optional ways to participate more deeply.

### 10.4 Resilience without exaggerated claims

Emergency preparedness and independent communication are important long-term aspirations.

Marketing must avoid promising uninterrupted communication, universal coverage, or emergency-service reliability before those capabilities have been demonstrated.
