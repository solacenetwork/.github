# SOLACE Scientific Research Topics

## 1. Purpose

SOLACE Network envisions communities participating in scientific exploration, experimentation, and the collection of useful research data.

This document preserves scientific questions, experimental concepts, and potential research subjects discussed during the development of SOLACE Network.

Topics may involve energy, agriculture, biology, environmental monitoring, computing, communication, and other fields.

The document serves as an idea catalog rather than a formal research program or development roadmap.

**Inclusion does not mean a concept has been scientifically validated, funded, approved, or scheduled for implementation.**

Research topics may eventually develop into independent projects with their own protocols, software, datasets, documentation, and published findings.

## 2. Community Science Philosophy

One of SOLACE Network's long-term goals is to make scientific participation accessible beyond universities and traditional research institutions.

Individuals and households may contribute useful observations, conduct small experiments, and collaborate with others investigating similar questions.

Thousands of participants collecting comparable measurements over time could potentially produce valuable datasets.

Examples include:

- Gardeners comparing cultivation results.
- Amateur naturalists documenting biodiversity.
- Households collecting local environmental measurements.
- Communities investigating renewable energy.
- Participants testing practical technologies under different operating conditions.

The objective is not to replace professional scientific research, but to provide infrastructure for meaningful participation and collaboration.

Research methods should emphasize reproducibility, accurate measurements, transparent limitations, and appropriate review.

## 3. Alternative Energy Generation

### 3.1 Human-powered electricity generation

**Concept:** Investigate electricity generation using pedal-driven mechanical systems.

Earlier discussions considered connecting several exercise bicycles or pedal stations to a shared generation and battery-charging system.

One illustrative concept involved three to five participants contributing power simultaneously.

Potential research questions:

- How much sustained electrical energy can participants realistically generate?
- What mechanical and electrical conversion losses occur?
- How practical is human-powered charging for low-power computing equipment?
- Could shared pedal-generation facilities serve both educational and practical purposes?
- What storage and voltage-regulation arrangements would be necessary?

The goal would be to measure real-world performance rather than assume that human power can reliably support continuous household loads.

### 3.2 Stirling engines

**Concept:** Investigate small Stirling engines using available temperature differences.

Previous brainstorming considered combining solar heating with relatively cool underground or geothermal environments.

Potential research questions:

- What temperature differences are achievable under realistic outdoor conditions?
- How much usable mechanical or electrical power could a small system produce?
- How does performance vary with weather and seasonal conditions?
- What heat-exchanger arrangements would be required?
- Is the energy output sufficient to justify the equipment and maintenance?

The practicality of this concept remains unverified.

### 3.3 Solar chimney generation

**Concept:** Investigate portable or small-scale solar chimney systems.

A solar chimney uses solar heating to create airflow through a structure.

Potential research questions:

- Can a compact system generate enough airflow for useful electrical production?
- What collector area and chimney height would be required?
- How does output vary during cloudy conditions or at different times of day?
- Could a modular or transportable structure be feasible?

Scale and efficiency may be significant limitations.

### 3.4 Trompe systems

**Concept:** Investigate the use of flowing water to compress air through a trompe.

The resulting compressed air could potentially support mechanical work or other energy applications.

Potential research questions:

- What water flow and vertical drop are necessary?
- How much compressed air could a small installation produce?
- Could recovered energy be used efficiently for electricity generation?
- How would terrain and seasonal water availability affect operation?
- What environmental and safety restrictions would apply?

This concept is primarily relevant to locations with suitable water resources.

### 3.5 Tesla turbines

**Concept:** Explore Tesla turbine designs for possible small-scale water-driven generation.

Potential research questions:

- How do disk spacing, rotational speed, and fluid flow affect output?
- How does efficiency compare with conventional small hydro turbines?
- What manufacturing tolerances are practical for small experimental systems?
- Can a low-cost prototype produce repeatable and useful measurements?

The concept should be evaluated experimentally rather than assumed to outperform established turbines.

### 3.6 Thermoelectric generators

**Concept:** Investigate modern thermoelectric generation using temperature differences.

Potential research questions:

- What useful output can be achieved with readily available modules?
- Which practical heat sources and heat sinks provide sufficient temperature differences?
- How much power can be produced continuously?
- Can such systems support sensors or low-power communications equipment?
- How do cost and efficiency compare with other generation methods?

This subject may be particularly relevant to low-power scientific instruments.

## 4. Mechanical Energy Storage

### 4.1 Spring and flywheel systems

**Concept:** Investigate mechanical energy storage using springs, flywheels, or combinations of the two.

An earlier idea involved an underground mechanical storage arrangement using a large spring and flywheel.

Potential research questions:

- How much energy can be stored in practical mechanical arrangements?
- What conversion losses occur during charging and discharge?
- How does stored energy compare with commercially available batteries?
- What maintenance requirements would apply?
- What containment and mechanical safety systems would be necessary?

High-energy rotating or tensioned mechanisms can present serious hazards. Any physical experimentation would require an appropriate engineering and safety assessment.

## 5. Energy Storage and Power Management

### 5.1 Modular battery systems

**Concept:** Study independently powered components instead of relying entirely on a single shared battery.

Potential subjects include:

- Separate battery packs for computing, networking, and communications equipment.
- Hot-swappable power systems.
- Automatic transfer between power sources.
- Graceful shutdown during low-power conditions.
- Prioritizing critical network services when stored energy is limited.

### 5.2 Reusing existing battery ecosystems

**Concept:** Evaluate the feasibility of using commercially available rechargeable tool batteries in community-operated equipment.

Potential research questions:

- Which battery types provide suitable voltage and capacity?
- What battery-management protections are required?
- How do replacement cost and availability compare with dedicated batteries?
- Can compatibility be reliably documented by manufacturer and model?
- What are the safety implications of continuous charging and discharge?

Detailed practical reference designs, if validated, would eventually belong in `HARDWARE.md`.

### 5.3 Low-voltage DC power systems

Earlier discussions explored supplying household network equipment through direct-current electrical systems.

Potential research questions:

- Can reducing conversion stages improve overall efficiency?
- Which standardized DC voltages would be appropriate?
- How can devices with different voltage requirements be supported safely?
- What protection and distribution mechanisms are necessary?

Any prototype would require appropriate electrical safety precautions.

## 6. Agriculture and Gardening Research

Gardening was one of the earliest proposed applications of SOLACE Network.

A major research opportunity is the collection of comparable observations from many participating households.

Potential research subjects include:

- Planting dates and growing seasons.
- Soil conditions and amendments.
- Temperature and rainfall.
- Irrigation practices.
- Plant growth and development.
- Pest and disease observations.
- Harvest timing and yields.
- Regional differences in growing outcomes.
- Comparison of cultivation methods.

A single household's observations can help improve its own garden.

When observations are consistently collected across many households, they may support broader analysis of regional growing conditions and cultivation results.

Detailed gardening application requirements belong in the `solace-garden` repository.

## 7. Mycology and Fungal Research

Earlier discussions explored documenting mushrooms and other fungi through photography, location observations, and potential analytical equipment.

### 7.1 Community fungal observations

Potential research activities include:

- Recording fungal species observations.
- Photographing specimens.
- Documenting seasonal occurrence.
- Comparing observations across habitats.
- Recording environmental conditions.
- Contributing observations to structured research datasets.

The accuracy and limitations of species identification would need to be recorded.

### 7.2 Portable mushroom analysis device

**Concept:** Investigate a portable, open-source research device combining several kinds of information.

Previously discussed components included:

- Photographic documentation.
- Geographic location.
- Chemical or other sample measurements.
- A structured fungal reference database.
- Optional smartphone connectivity.
- Computer-assisted comparison with suspected species.
- Expert review of uncertain identifications.

The proposed approach involved users selecting a suspected species and comparing evidence against that hypothesis.

The device was envisioned as a research instrument, not an authoritative edible-mushroom identification system.

**The device must not be represented as determining whether a mushroom is safe to eat.**

Reliable species identification and toxicity assessment are separate scientific problems. An incorrect result could have serious consequences.

This concept remains exploratory and would require validated methods, reference samples, and expert oversight before practical use.

## 8. Environmental Monitoring

### 8.1 Local weather observations

**Concept:** Collect weather observations from participating households and neighborhoods.

Potential measurements include:

- Air temperature.
- Relative humidity.
- Rainfall.
- Atmospheric pressure.
- Wind conditions.
- Soil temperature.
- Soil moisture.

Possible research questions:

- How do microclimates vary within a neighborhood?
- How do local measurements compare with regional weather stations?
- Can localized observations improve understanding of gardening conditions?
- How consistent are readings from different low-cost sensors?

### 8.2 Sensor accuracy and calibration

Community research involving inexpensive sensors requires attention to measurement quality.

Potential research subjects include:

- Calibration methods.
- Sensor drift.
- Environmental exposure.
- Consistency between device models.
- Data quality flags.
- Long-term reliability.

The goal is to distinguish useful measurements from misleading or poorly calibrated data.

### 8.3 Environmental and biodiversity observations

Other potential community research subjects include:

- Seasonal vegetation changes.
- Wildlife observations.
- Fungal diversity.
- Changes in local growing conditions.
- Relationships between weather and biological activity.

Individual research projects would need appropriately designed observation protocols.

## 9. Network and Communication Research

Some networking questions may also support collaborative experiments.

Potential subjects include:

- Wireless signal propagation across different terrain.
- Effects of vegetation and buildings on radio connections.
- Network reliability under varying weather conditions.
- Power consumption of network equipment.
- Performance of low-bandwidth radio technologies.
- Practical coverage measurements.
- Connectivity between household and neighborhood equipment.

These experiments may inform later SOLACE networking standards.

However, experimental communications research is separate from committed network software features.

Operational architecture belongs in `NETWORK_OVERVIEW.md`, while implementation plans belong in the appropriate technical specifications.

## 10. Community Data Collection

The broader research vision includes a common framework for gathering observations from many participants.

Potential capabilities include:

- Structured research forms.
- Consistent measurement units.
- Standardized observation metadata.
- Optional geographic information.
- Photographic and sensor data.
- Quality-control procedures.
- Exportable datasets.
- Research project documentation.
- Participant-controlled data-sharing preferences.

The long-term concept includes datasets assembled from large numbers of participating households.

Earlier discussions envisioned participation potentially reaching hundreds of thousands or even a million contributors.

These numbers are aspirational, not projected enrollment or established research capacity.

## 11. Research Data Stewardship

The proposed SOLACE Foundation may eventually help organize, preserve, and share collective scientific datasets.

One idea discussed was making qualifying research data available for nonprofit and scientific purposes while licensing certain commercial uses to help support the Foundation.

Any such program would require clearly defined:

- Participant consent.
- Data ownership and licensing.
- Privacy protections.
- Data quality and provenance.
- Access controls.
- Research ethics.
- Commercial licensing policies.
- Procedures for correcting or withdrawing data where applicable.

These policies have not been finalized.

## 12. From Research Topic to Project

The topics in this document are exploratory.

If sufficient interest develops around a subject, it could become a separate SOLACE research project.

A future research project could include:

1. A clearly defined research question.
2. Existing background knowledge and references.
3. Testable hypotheses.
4. An experimental or observational method.
5. Equipment and measurement requirements.
6. Safety and ethical considerations.
7. Procedures for documenting results.
8. A reproducible dataset and analysis.
9. Findings, limitations, and proposed follow-up research.

The initial topic list does not establish these protocols or imply that experiments have already been conducted.

## 13. Research Priorities

No formal research priorities have been established.

Agriculture, environmental observations, and practical network measurements may provide accessible opportunities for early community participation.

More complex experimental energy and analytical-instrument concepts would require additional expertise, equipment, funding, and validation.

Research projects should develop independently of the core SOLACE software roadmap unless a specific dependency is deliberately approved.
