# SOLACE Network

### Live Well Together.

**SOLACE** stands for **Sustainable Open-Source Lab Augmented Community Example**.

SOLACE Network is an open-source initiative developing software, hardware standards, and community tools to help families and neighborhoods connect, communicate, organize, teach, learn, and work together.

We believe technology should empower individuals and communities, encourage cooperation, and promote greater independence.

Our goal is to provide a modular framework that people can adapt to their own needs, using affordable hardware, open standards, and freely available software.

## What We're Building

SOLACE Network is designed around several interconnected ideas:

- **Households and neighborhoods** — Tools for organizing households, connecting neighbors, sharing resources, and building stronger communities.
- **Education and knowledge** — Applications for learning, teaching, research, and sharing information.
- **Community science** — Tools for collecting, organizing, and sharing scientific observations and research data.
- **Local-first computing** — Applications and services that can operate locally, even when internet connectivity is limited or unavailable.
- **Decentralized networking** — A long-term vision for connecting households and neighborhoods through independent, community-operated networks.

## Open by Design

SOLACE Network embraces open-source software, open standards, and a bring-your-own-device philosophy.

Our goal isn't to require everyone to purchase specialized equipment. Instead, we want to develop compatible tools and reference implementations that people can build, modify, and operate themselves.

The project is being developed incrementally, beginning with foundational applications and services and evolving toward a decentralized network.

**SOLACE Network is in the early planning and development stages.**

Our repositories contain a combination of architectural documentation, experiments, and emerging applications. Many of the capabilities described in our documentation are long-term goals rather than completed features.

We welcome interest from people who believe that technology can help communities become more connected, resilient, and self-reliant.
